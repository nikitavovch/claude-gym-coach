# Zercher Squat (Зерхер-присед)

## Setup

- Bar sits in the crease of the elbows, arms wrapped around it, hands clasped or gripping the bar itself.
- A folded pad or towel around the bar at the elbow contact point makes a real difference in tolerating the load.
- Stance similar to a front squat — a bit narrower, upright torso.

## Cues

- Keep the torso as upright as the movement allows — inherently more upright than a back squat.
- Brace hard before descending; the bar position makes bracing feel different (more anterior pressure) than a back or front squat.
- Elbows stay tucked close to the ribcage rather than flaring out.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "My elbows/forearms hurt from the bar" → add more padding, or shorten range of motion/reduce load while the tissue adapts — a load-tolerance issue, not usually a technique flaw.
- "I feel like I fold forward at the bottom" → keep the brace tight and consciously keep the chest lifted through the bottom position.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Is the limiting factor the legs/hips, or the elbows/forearms giving out from the bar contact?

## Safety notes

- Zercher squats are usually done without a rack (deadlifted or cleaned into position) — no pin bail-out. If a rep fails, squat the bar back down to the floor under control or drop it forward; don't try to muscle through.
- Because of the arm position, this is a poor exercise to max out alone without a clear plan for what a failed rep looks like.
