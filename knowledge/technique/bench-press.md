# Bench Press (Жим штанги лёжа)

*Variants covered by this card: Pause Bench Press / Жим штанги лёжа с паузой, Pin Press (Bench) / Жим лёжа от пинов.*

## Setup

- Eyes roughly under the bar, shoulder blades pulled together and down ("pinch a pencil between your shoulder blades"), a slight to moderate upper-back arch is normal.
- Feet planted flat and stable — wherever they land that lets the lifter push into the floor without the hips lifting off the bench.
- Grip width comfortable for the individual's shoulders — forearms roughly vertical at the bottom is a common starting point, with real individual variation.
- Un-rack by pressing the bar up and out over the shoulders/eyes, not pulling it out at an angle.

## Cues

- Lower the bar to a consistent spot on the torso (lower chest for most flat-bench setups) under control.
- Keep the shoulder blades pinned together and down through the descent — this protects the shoulder and shortens the effective range of motion.
- Drive the bar up and slightly back toward the face/throat rather than straight up — the more mechanically efficient bar path once the bar leaves the chest.
- Keep the feet driving into the floor throughout for stability (leg drive) without letting the hips come up off the bench.
- Breathe in before descending and hold through the hardest part of the rep, releasing near lockout.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "The bar feels like it wanders forward toward my face or backward toward my neck instead of a smooth path" → focus on the "up and back toward the throat" bar path once the bar leaves the chest, and recheck the touch point on the chest is consistent rep to rep.
- "My shoulders pinch or hurt near the bottom of the rep" → recheck scapular retraction is held throughout the descent (not just at setup), and consider a slightly higher touch point or narrower/wider grip — this is one of the more common self-reported bench issues and usually responds to a grip or touch-point adjustment.
- "My butt comes off the bench when I push hard" → bring the feet in slightly and drive forward through the floor rather than straight down, which reduces the tendency to lift the hips.
- "One side goes up faster than the other" → check grip width is even on both hands and the bar starts perfectly centered before un-racking — uneven bar paths are very commonly just a setup symmetry issue.
- "I get a cramp in my back or hips when I set up a big arch" → common when new to arching and usually fades with practice; stretching beforehand can help, or a slightly smaller arch is a reasonable trade-off outside competition.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Is the shoulder discomfort at the bottom of the rep, at the top, or throughout?
- Does the bar path feel like it drifts toward your face, or back toward your neck/rack?
- Is one arm/side noticeably different from the other, or does it feel symmetrical?

## Safety notes

- Always bench with a spotter for near-maximal work, or with the rack's safety pins/straps set just below chest height if training alone — never bench heavy, alone, with no pins and no spotter.
- If a rep is failing with no spotter present: roll the bar down onto the torso and slide/sit up and out from under it, or tip one side down to dump the plates — decide and rehearse this before it's needed, not in the moment.
- Brace and breathe per `_general.md`.
- Stop the set on sharp shoulder pain, as distinct from ordinary muscular fatigue/burn.

## Variant notes

- **Pause Bench Press** — identical setup/cues, with a dead stop (no bounce off the chest) held 1–2 seconds at the bottom before pressing. If tightness is lost during the pause, actively hold the brace and scapular position rather than relaxing into it.
- **Pin Press (Bench)** — pressed from a dead stop off pins set at a chosen height (often just above the chest), removing the stretch reflex entirely; same press-up cues, but expect the weight to feel notably harder off the bottom than the same load pressed from the chest.
