# Back Squat (Присед со штангой на спине)

*Variants covered by this card: Pause Squat / Присед со штангой с паузой, Pin Squat / Присед от пинов, Box Squat / Присед в бокс, Safety Bar Squat / Присед со SSB-грифом.*

## Setup

- Bar position: high-bar (on the traps) or low-bar (on the rear delts, across the scapular spine) — either is legitimate; they change torso angle and which muscles carry more load, not "correct vs incorrect."
- Foot position: tripod contact (base of big toe, base of little toe, heel), weight felt evenly across all three. Stance width and toe-out wherever the lifter can hit full depth without knee pain.
- Grip as narrow as shoulder mobility comfortably allows — helps create upper-back tightness, but shoulder pain trumps that.
- Un-rack by standing the bar up and stepping back only 2–3 steps.
- Brace before every rep: a deep diaphragmatic breath, then tension "like bracing for a punch to the stomach" (not a crunch).

## Cues

- Feel for hip tension before descending: "screw your feet into the floor" (narrower stances) or "spread the floor apart" (wider stances) — both cue active external rotation torque at the hip.
- Keep weight balanced across the whole foot on the way down, not rocking onto toes or heels.
- Descend under control to the deepest position the lower back can hold in its normal, moderately-arched-to-neutral shape.
- Drive up by pushing the floor away — not by "leading with the hips" or "leading with the chest" specifically; whichever keeps the bar path efficient for that person.
- Keep the brace and breath held through the sticking point; exhale near or after lockout.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "The bar feels like it's pulling me forward and I have to lean into it" → recheck upper-back tightness ("try to bend the bar across your shoulders") and confirm the bar sits in the same groove every rep; a bar migrating toward the neck forces a forward lean to compensate.
- "My knees feel like they cave inward, especially coming out of the bottom" → narrow the stance slightly, or cue "push your knees out toward your pinky toes" during the drive; band-around-the-knees drilling can retrain this over a few weeks. Some inward knee travel is normal and not automatically a problem — see the knees-over-toes note in `_general.md`.
- "My lower back rounds or gives right at the bottom" (butt wink) → stop the descent an inch or two above where the rounding starts and treat that as working depth for now; revisit as hip/ankle mobility and control improve. See the butt-wink note in `_general.md`.
- "I lose my air / feel light-headed partway through a heavy set" → check whether the breath is held for the whole set instead of reset between reps; a fresh breath at the top of each rep (bar still loaded, torso still tight) is normal on multi-rep sets.
- "The bar feels unstable / wants to roll on my back" → double-check hand and bar placement before unracking every set, not just the first.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- When it feels unstable or wrong, is that at the top, in the middle of the descent, or right at the bottom?
- Does anything hurt, or does it just feel awkward/unfamiliar? (Awkward is normal to troubleshoot with cues; pain is a different conversation.)
- Is this happening on every rep of the set, or only on the last one or two hard reps?

## Safety notes

- Always squat inside a rack with the safety pins/straps set to catch the bar near the bottom of the intended range — the single most important habit for training alone.
- If a rep is failing: sit back and down onto the pins/straps (or dump the bar backward off the shoulders in a power rack without a front guard), never fight it back up or twist sideways out of it.
- A spotter is optional if pins are set correctly; without pins (e.g. platform squatting), a spotter or a clear bail-out plan is not optional near max loads.
- Brace and breathe per `_general.md`.
- Stop the set on pain (not simple fatigue or burn), or if bar control is clearly degrading rep to rep.

## Variant notes

- **Pause Squat** — same setup/cues; hold a dead stop (no bounce) at the bottom for 1–3 seconds before driving up. If tightness is lost during the pause, actively re-brace during it rather than waiting it out passively.
- **Pin Squat** — dead stop off safety pins at a chosen height; same drive-up mechanics but zero stretch reflex — the weight will feel harder off the bottom than the same load in a regular squat.
- **Box Squat** — sit back to lightly touch (not collapse onto) a box at target depth, then drive up; cue "touch and go," keeping the brace on through the touch rather than relaxing onto the box.
- **Safety Bar Squat** — the yoke-style bar shifts the center of mass forward, so expect and allow slightly more forward torso lean than a low-bar squat; same knee/hip/back cues otherwise.
