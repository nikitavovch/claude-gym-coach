# Bent-Over Row (Тяга штанги в наклоне)

## Setup

- Hinge at the hips to a torso angle the lifter can hold with a flat-to-neutral back (commonly somewhere between 45° and near-horizontal), knees softly bent.
- Grip just outside shoulder width, arms hanging straight down at the start.
- Brace the torso before initiating, similar to a Romanian deadlift setup.

## Cues

- Pull the bar toward the lower ribcage/upper stomach, driving the elbows back and slightly up rather than straight out to the sides.
- Keep the torso angle constant through the set — the movement should come from the arms and shoulder blades, not from the torso swinging up and down to help.
- Squeeze the shoulder blades together at the top of each pull before controlling the bar back down.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "I feel my lower back working more than my back muscles" → recheck the torso brace and hip-hinge position — a torso that isn't braced, or a back already rounding at the start, shifts strain to the lower back.
- "My torso swings up and down to help move the weight" → reduce the load and/or consciously lock the torso angle, using momentum-free reps to relearn the position before adding weight back.
- "I feel it mostly in my arms, not my back" → focus on driving the elbows back rather than curling the weight up, and consciously pull with the shoulder blades first.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Does your torso stay at the same angle throughout the set, or does it move up and down noticeably as the weight gets pulled?
- Where do you feel the effort most — lower back, arms, or upper back?

## Safety notes

- If the lower back is the limiting factor or a known sensitive area, a chest-supported alternative removes that demand entirely — a reasonable substitution, not a failure to "do the real exercise."
- No pin/rack bail-out applies here; if a rep can't be completed, simply lower the bar back to the floor under control.
