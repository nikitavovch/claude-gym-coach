# Sumo Deadlift (Становая тяга сумо)

## Setup

- Wide stance, toes turned out enough that the shins can stay vertical once hips are set.
- Grip inside the legs, narrower than the stance — hands roughly shoulder-width or just outside it.
- Hips set lower and closer to the bar than in a conventional pull, torso more upright.
- Same bracing routine: pull the slack out of the bar, deep breath, brace.

## Cues

- Actively push the knees out toward the toes as tension is taken on the bar, before the pull starts.
- Think about pushing the floor apart with the feet as the pull begins, similar to the "spread the floor" squat cue.
- Torso stays more upright throughout than a conventional pull; keep the chest lifted rather than leading with the hips rising.
- Lock out by driving the hips through to full extension, not leaning back excessively.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "My knees cave in as the bar leaves the floor" → reinforce "push the knees out" as the very first cue on setup and re-apply it as tension builds, before the bar breaks the floor.
- "My hips shoot up and my back rounds" → recheck the hips aren't set too high to begin with; keep the torso lifted by actively driving the legs rather than the back.
- "I feel like I'm fighting the bar around my thighs on the way up" → double-check grip and stance width — a stance too narrow for the grip (or vice versa) creates unnecessary friction and a longer bar path around the legs.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Is the sticking point right off the floor, or somewhere around knee height?
- Do the knees cave in before the bar moves, or only after it's already moving?

## Safety notes

- Same bail-out logic as conventional deadlift: set the bar back down under control if a rep stalls; no spotter can meaningfully assist mid-pull.
- Brace and breathe per `_general.md`; stop on sharp/radiating pain rather than pushing through it.
