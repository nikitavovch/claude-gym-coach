# Hanging Leg Raise (Подъём ног в висе)

*Also covers: Ab Wheel Rollout / Ролик для пресса — different equipment, same core-bracing principle.*

## Setup

- Hanging leg raise: full grip on a pull-up bar, hanging with control (not swinging) before starting.
- Ab wheel: kneeling, hands on the wheel's handles, starting position with the torso upright over the knees.

## Cues

- Hanging leg raise: raise the legs (straight or bent at the knee, depending on the variation/difficulty level) by curling the pelvis up and under rather than just swinging the legs forward from the hips.
- Ab wheel: roll out under control only as far as the lower back can stay braced and flat — the point where the lower back starts to sag or arch is the working range limit for that day.
- Both: keep the core actively braced throughout rather than letting momentum do the work.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "I swing my legs to get them up" (hanging leg raise) → slow the tempo and focus on curling the pelvis rather than kicking the legs forward; reducing range (knee raises instead of straight-leg raises) is a reasonable regression.
- "My lower back sags/arches toward the floor" (ab wheel) → reduce the range of motion — roll out less far — and keep the core actively braced instead of letting the hips drop.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- For the ab wheel: does the lower back stay flat throughout, or does it dip toward the floor at the furthest point of the roll-out?
- For hanging leg raises: are the legs being lifted with control, or does it take some swing/momentum to get them moving?

## Safety notes

- Ab wheel rollouts in particular can strain the lower back if the range of motion outpaces core control — building range gradually over weeks, rather than chasing a full rollout immediately, is the main safety lever here.
- For hanging leg raises, grip fatigue giving out unexpectedly is the main practical risk — same note as pull-ups.
