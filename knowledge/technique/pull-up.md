# Pull-Up (Подтягивания прямым хватом)

*Also covers: Chin-Up / Подтягивания обратным хватом — same cues, different grip.*

## Setup

- Overhand, wider-than-shoulder grip for pull-ups; underhand, shoulder-width-ish grip for chin-ups.
- Start from a full dead hang, shoulders relaxed down before initiating (not a partial hang with the shoulders already shrugged up).

## Cues

- Initiate the pull by drawing the shoulder blades down and back before bending the arms significantly.
- Pull until the chin clears the bar (or the chest approaches the bar, depending on the standard being used), keeping the body relatively controlled rather than swinging or kipping (unless that's an intentional, separate training goal).
- Lower under control back to a full dead hang each rep.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "I swing my legs / use momentum to get up" → slow the tempo and focus on the dead-hang-to-shoulder-blade-engagement sequencing before pulling with the arms. If a swing is needed to complete reps, that means the load (bodyweight) currently exceeds strict strength — an assisted variation is the appropriate regression, not "more willpower."
- "My shoulders hurt at the bottom of the hang" → check whether the bottom position is a fully relaxed, shrugged-up dead hang versus a controlled hang with the shoulder blades gently engaged — a completely passive, shrugged hang under repeated load is a common source of shoulder discomfort for some lifters.
- "I can't get more than a rep or two" → a normal starting point for a hard bodyweight exercise — assisted pull-up machines, bands, or a lighter accessory (lat pulldown) are all legitimate ways to build toward more reps rather than grinding failed attempts.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Is the shoulder discomfort at the very bottom of the hang, at the top near the bar, or throughout the whole rep?
- Are you completing reps with a controlled, arms-only pull, or does it take a leg swing/kip to get up?

## Safety notes

- Dropping down from a bar after a set is fine at a normal working height; land softly (bent knees) rather than locking the knees on landing.
- If training to true failure, be aware of grip giving out unexpectedly — that's the main injury risk (a sudden drop), more than the pulling motion itself.
