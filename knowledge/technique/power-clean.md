# Power Clean (Силовой подъём штанги на грудь)

## Setup

- Bar over mid-foot, stance and grip similar to a conventional deadlift but hands slightly wider (just outside the legs), hook grip if the lifter has learned it.
- Hips a bit lower than a conventional deadlift start, shoulders over or slightly ahead of the bar.
- Brace and take the slack out of the bar the same way as a deadlift before pulling.

## Cues

- The pull happens in phases: a controlled first pull off the floor (similar tempo/feel to a deadlift) that accelerates explosively once the bar passes the knees (the "second pull").
- Extend the hips, knees and ankles aggressively at the top of the pull ("triple extension") — this is meant to be explosive and fast, not a slow grind like a max-effort deadlift.
- Pull the body under the bar and catch it on the front of the shoulders in a front-rack position with elbows up, absorbing the catch with the legs (a partial squat).
- Finish by standing to full hip extension with the bar racked on the shoulders.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "I'm basically doing a really fast deadlift and then muscling the bar up with my arms" → usually means the "second pull" leg/hip extension isn't being used — refocus on driving through the floor explosively once the bar passes the knees, rather than pulling with the arms.
- "I catch the bar way out in front of me and have to lunge forward" → work on pulling the body under the bar faster rather than trying to pull the bar further; the catch should feel like the bar meets a body already dropped under it, not a bar muscled up high enough to walk under.
- "My wrists/front rack position hurts" → very commonly a mobility-tolerance issue with the front-rack wrist position — reducing load, wrist wraps, or dedicated front-rack mobility work are all reasonable responses; not necessarily a pulling-technique flaw.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Does the bar feel like it's being muscled up with the arms, or does it feel like it "floats" up after an aggressive leg/hip drive?
- Is the catch position uncomfortable because of the wrists/front rack, or because you're not getting under the bar fast enough?

## Safety notes

- The most technically demanding lift in this library. For a lifter with no coaching history in Olympic-lift technique, strongly recommend starting very light (empty-bar-or-near-empty practice) and, ideally, in-person coaching or well-reviewed video instruction before adding real load — say plainly that a text-only coach is a poor sole teacher for this specific lift.
- If a rep is failed or feels out of control at the top, bail by pushing the bar away and stepping back/down — don't chase it forward or backward.
- Train on a platform with room to bail the bar forward, and avoid attempting this lift for the first time under heavy fatigue.
