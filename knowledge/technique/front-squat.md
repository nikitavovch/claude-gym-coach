# Front Squat (Фронтальный присед)

## Setup

- Bar rests across the front delts, either in a clean-grip rack (fingers under the bar, elbows high) or crossed-arm grip — whichever suits the lifter's wrist/shoulder mobility.
- Elbows up and pointing forward throughout setup — this, not grip strength, is what keeps the bar from rolling off the shoulders.
- Stance similar to back squat, usually a touch narrower and more upright.
- Brace the same way as back squat before un-racking.

## Cues

- Keep elbows high through the whole rep, not just at the top — elbows dropping is usually the first thing to go wrong under fatigue.
- Torso stays more upright than a back squat by necessity — cue "sit down between your heels" rather than "sit back."
- Full brace and breath held the same way as back squat.
- Drive up keeping the elbows lifted so the bar doesn't roll forward off the shoulders.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "The bar rolls forward off my shoulders near the bottom" → focus on keeping elbows pointed forward and up throughout, especially at the bottom where fatigue drops them first.
- "My wrists hurt in the clean grip" → try the crossed-arm grip instead, or a strap/towel trick around the bar. Wrist mobility for the clean grip can be built separately, but there's no requirement to use that grip.
- "I feel like I tip forward at the bottom" → check the torso stays close to vertical rather than hinging forward like a back squat; a narrower stance sometimes helps.
- "My upper back rounds under the bar" → same upper-back tightness cue as back squat ("bend the bar") — a front squat needs at least as much thoracic extension to keep the bar supported.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Is the discomfort in the wrists, the front of the shoulders, or the upper back?
- Does the bar feel like it's rolling forward, or does the whole torso feel like it's tipping forward?

## Safety notes

- Same rack pin height and bail-out logic as back squat; because the bar is in front, a failed rep is usually easier to simply drop forward and step back from — a legitimate way to bail, not a failure.
- Brace and breathe per `_general.md`.
- Stop the set on real pain, not just the awkwardness of an unfamiliar bar position.
