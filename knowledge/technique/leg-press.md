# Leg Press (Жим ногами)

## Setup

- Feet shoulder-width (or the stance the lifter finds most comfortable) on the platform, roughly centered.
- Lower back and hips stay in contact with the pad/seat throughout — this is the main setup priority.
- Safety catches/stops set appropriately before starting, per the specific machine.

## Cues

- Lower the platform under control to a depth where the lower back stays flat against the pad — the moment the hips start to round or lift off the pad is the effective depth limit for that person on that day.
- Drive back up through the whole foot, not just the toes.
- Avoid locking the knees out aggressively and bouncing into the next rep.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "My lower back/hips lift off the pad at the bottom" → reduce the range of motion (don't lower as far) — the single most common fix for this symptom on a leg press.
- "My knees feel like they cave in" → recheck foot placement width and try actively pushing the knees out in line with the toes during the drive.
- "I feel it in my lower back instead of my legs" → same as above — the range of motion is very likely going past the point where the hips stay anchored to the pad.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Do your hips/lower back stay flat against the pad through the whole range, or do they lift/round at the bottom?

## Safety notes

- Always confirm the safety catches are set and functional before loading the sled; know how to engage them from inside the machine if a rep needs to be bailed.
- Never let the knees fully lock and bounce under load — a hyperextended locked-knee bounce under a heavy sled is a real knee-injury mechanism.
