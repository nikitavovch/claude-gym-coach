# Conventional Deadlift (Становая тяга (классическая))

*Variant covered by this card: Deficit Deadlift / Становая тяга с дефицита.*

## Setup

- Bar over mid-foot, shins close to (nearly touching) the bar.
- Grip just outside the legs — narrow enough that the arms lightly brush the thighs, not fight against them.
- Hips set by pushing them back until the shins touch the bar and the back is in its normal, flat-to-slightly-arched position — not a fixed hip-height rule.
- Take the slack out of the bar before pulling: pull up gently until the bar audibly/visibly comes tight against the plates before the real pull starts.
- Deep breath into the belly and brace before initiating the pull.

## Cues

- Think "push the floor away" rather than "pull the bar up" — keeps the whole body moving together instead of the arms/back initiating alone.
- Keep the bar in contact with (or grazing) the legs the entire way up — "drag the bar up your shins and thighs."
- Keep the lats engaged: "put your shoulder blades in your back pocket" or "bend the bar around your legs" — same tension, different phrasing.
- Hips and shoulders rise at the same rate — neither the hips shooting up first nor the shoulders staying pinned while hips rise.
- Finish standing fully tall, squeezing the glutes, without leaning back past vertical.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "My hips shoot up first and my back feels like it's doing all the work early in the pull" → refocus on "push the floor away with the legs" as the very first movement; recheck the hips aren't set too high at the start.
- "The bar drifts forward away from my shins as it comes up" → actively pull the bar back into the legs the whole way up and recheck lat tension in the setup — a forward drift usually means the lats let go partway through.
- "My lower back rounds noticeably, especially right off the floor" → reset hip height in the starting position (often too low, forcing a rounded back to reach the bar) and re-cue "chest up, hips back" before initiating.
- "My arms feel like they're doing the pulling" (biceps strain, especially on a mixed grip) → grip as hard as possible but keep the arms passive — "hang" from a tight grip rather than rowing the bar.
- "I lose my grip before my legs/back are the limiting factor" → straps for volume/accessory work, alternating pronated/supinated hand on a mixed grip, or dedicated grip work — a legitimate limiter to train around, not a technique fault.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- When the back rounds, is that right at the floor, or partway up once the bar passes the knees?
- Is the limiting factor grip giving out, or legs/back running out of strength?
- Does anything hurt, versus it just feeling heavy and grinding (expected on a top set)?

## Safety notes

- No rack or pins to bail onto — if a rep is failing, set the bar back down under control (or let it drop from near lockout), never keep straining against a stalled bar.
- A spotter cannot meaningfully assist a deadlift the way they can a squat or bench — for a true near-max attempt, someone present is about safety supervision, not physical help.
- Brace and breathe per `_general.md`; reset the breath at the top of the rep or with the bar on the floor between reps, never mid-pull.
- Stop the set on sharp or radiating pain (especially anything shooting down a leg), as opposed to the normal heavy, effortful grind of a top set.

## Variant notes

- **Deficit Deadlift** — standing on a small platform (commonly 2.5–7.5 cm / 1–3 in) increases range of motion from the floor. Same setup/pull cues; typically requires reducing working load versus a regular-height deadlift, and a rounding low back off the floor is more likely to show up here first if it's going to show up at all.
- **Pause Deadlift** — not documented separately in the source research `[expert default, unverified — inferred by analogy with the Pause Squat variant, not from a Pause Deadlift-specific source]`. Treat it like other paused main-lift variants: same setup and pull cues, hold the dead stop actively (re-brace, don't relax) rather than passively waiting, and expect the pull to feel harder off the pause than the same load pulled continuously.
