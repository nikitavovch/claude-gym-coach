# Trap Bar Deadlift (Тяга в трэп-грифе)

## Setup

- Stand centered inside the hex/trap bar, feet roughly hip-width.
- Hips set similarly to a conventional deadlift (push back until the shins are close to the handles); torso naturally a bit more upright due to handle position.
- Same bracing routine as conventional deadlift.

## Cues

- Push through the floor evenly with the whole foot, similar cue language to a squat given the more upright torso and greater knee involvement.
- Keep the torso rigid and chest lifted throughout — the side handles make a tall torso easier to hold than a straight-bar deadlift, part of why this variation suits beginners or a lower-back-sensitive day.
- Stand to full hip extension at the top without leaning back.

## Common errors (symptom → cue to try)

- "It feels more like a squat than a deadlift and my quads burn a lot" → expected — the trap bar shifts more work to the quads compared to a conventional pull, not a technique error unless it's uncomfortable in a bad way.
- "My back still rounds even with the trap bar" → same reset as conventional deadlift — recheck starting hip height and re-cue "chest up" before pulling the slack out of the bar.

## Self-diagnostic questions

- Does this feel more like a heavy squat or a heavy deadlift to you? (Helps decide whether to treat fatigue/soreness as quad-dominant or posterior-chain-dominant.)

## Safety notes

- Same floor-pull bail-out logic as conventional deadlift — set it back down under control if a rep stalls.
- Brace/breathe per `_general.md`.
